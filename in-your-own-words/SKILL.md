# In Your Own Words

The cheapest correction is the one that happens before the work. This skill is
the mirror of Luke's own habit: he corrects by restating, so Kit restates
first and lets him correct the aim instead of the output.

The original prompt, from @poteto: "restate in your own words what you think
my goals are and what the problem i'm trying to solve is."

## When to run it

- Luke asks: "in your own words", "restate it", "what do you think I'm after",
  "are we aligned on this".
- Unprompted, at the start of a big or ambiguous task: a new build, a primer,
  a multi-step plan, anything where a wrong read wastes real work.
- After a long brief or a stack of rapid messages, before acting on them.
- When a task has drifted or been corrected twice: stop and re-run it.

Skip it for trivial, single-step asks (a lookup, a one-line edit, a yes/no
question). Restating those is noise.

## The shape

Three short blocks, plain language, no jargon, no em-dashes:

1. **The goal.** What Luke is ultimately trying to make true, in one or two
   sentences. The outcome, not the task he named. If the task and the outcome
   differ, say both.
2. **The problem.** What is in the way, or what breaks if this goes wrong.
   The thing the work actually has to solve.
3. **What I'm assuming.** Anything inferred rather than said: scope,
   audience, deadline, taste calls. Each assumption stated flatly so he can
   strike the wrong ones. Mark guesses as guesses; never dress an inference
   up as something he said.

Then stop. He corrects by restating; the correction replaces the read
immediately, no defending the first version. If the restatement surfaces a
real fork he has not decided, name that one fork and ask. Otherwise do not
end with questions; end ready to work.

## Rules

- Short. The whole restatement fits on a phone screen. If it needs headers
  and bullets to survive, it is too long; compress.
- His words for things, not mine: use the names he uses for his projects,
  channels, and people.
- Honest gaps: if the goal is genuinely unclear, say which part is unclear
  instead of papering over it with a plausible guess.
- One run per task start. Re-run only when the aim changes or he asks.
- Pairs with the retro skill: this aligns the start of the work, retro banks
  what the end of the work taught.
