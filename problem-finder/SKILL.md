# Problem Finder

Find business-worthy problems and vet them BEFORE pitching. Check first, pitch only survivors.

## The workflow

**Step 1 — Generate candidates in the right territory.**
Prefer physical, fragmented, unsexy markets: trades, field services, specialty cleanup, logistics, compliance-heavy niches. The standing heuristic (earned 2026-10-07 over ~50 vetted ideas): *paperwork problems attract SaaS founders; physical ones don't.* If every founder can picture the user at a desk, it's probably crowded.

**Step 2 — Demand validation (from the KDP playbook, adapted).**
- Google Trends: 10+ years of data, flat or rising only. Falling interest = dying problem.
- Existing spend: are people already paying for a bad solution? (The Amazon-bestseller-rank analog: bestseller rank <100,000 meant real buyers. Here: incumbents with real pricing pages and reviews mean real budgets.)
- Niche down via review-gap mining: read 1-star reviews of the incumbent. The wedge is always in the complaints.

**Step 3 — Competition check (1-2 web searches per candidate).**
Search "[vertical] software" / "[vertical] app". Name names and prices. Verdicts:
- **OPEN** — no credible purpose-built player. Rare.
- **HALF-SERVED** — players exist but miss the real wedge. Name the wedge precisely (e.g. "they do scheduling; nobody does the insurance documentation").
- **CROWDED** — kill it, one line, name the owner. Never pitch these.

**Step 4 — Pitch only survivors.**
Each survivor gets: the gap in 2-3 sentences, the business model (price + why that price), and honest TAM (a $2-5M ARR ceiling niche is fine — say so).

**Step 5 — Demand-trend check on survivors (mandatory, added 2026-10-07 after Luke caught the gap).**
Competition checks alone are not enough. For every survivor, check Google Trends (10-year window, US): flat or rising = green light; falling = kill or flag; spiky/seasonal = note it in the model (seasonal pricing). Never pitch a survivor without this.

## Vetting lenses

- **The YC filter:** would YC fund it? If yes, it's probably crowded or about to be. YC needs $100M+ outcomes, so they cluster in horizontal platforms, infra, deep tech. Our hunting ground is what they structurally ignore.
- **The QuoteIQ test:** is it standard field-service workflow (dispatch, quoting, invoicing)? If yes, an aggressive verticalizer probably owns it. Surviving whitespace is the non-standard workflow: insurance documentation, permit tracking, compliance dossiers.
- **The payer test:** who pays, and what breaks if they don't? "Insurer denies the claim" and "DOL fines you $1,409 per violation" beat "it'd be nice to be organized."
- **The layer reframe:** if the vertical is crowded, don't replace the legacy system — build the modern layer on top (family portal on funeral software, booking layer on tow software). Faster to build, easier to sell, no rip-and-replace fight.
- **TAM honesty:** small niches are allowed. State the ceiling plainly instead of inflating it.
